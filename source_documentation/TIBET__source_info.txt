TIBETAN GIVEN NAMES — SOURCE INFORMATION
=========================================
Language family: Sino-Tibetan, Tibetic branch (distinct from Tibeto-Burman)
Script: Tibetan script (Uchen); romanisation varies widely (Wylie, phonetic, etc.)

IMPORTANT CAVEAT — NO FREQUENCY DATA AVAILABLE
-----------------------------------------------
No publicly accessible dataset with population-level frequency counts for
Tibetan given names was found. This is explained by several structural factors:

1. Official Chinese government statistics for Tibet (TAR) do not disaggregate
   name frequency data by ethnicity in accessible public datasets.
2. The exile Tibetan community (~100,000 people, India/Nepal/West) does not
   have a centralised civil registration database that is publicly published.
3. No academic NLP corpus specifically targeting Tibetan personal name
   frequency was identified (the Tibetan NLP community focuses on text/speech,
   not name statistics).
4. Forebears.io does not have a dedicated Tibet/Tibetan forenames page with
   ranked frequency data comparable to other countries.

The file tibetan_names.csv therefore provides a curated reference list of
well-attested common Tibetan names with meanings and script forms, compiled
from the most reliable available sources.

------------------------------------------------------------
KEY CULTURAL NOTES
------------------------------------------------------------
- Tibetan names typically have NO family/surname component (except
  aristocratic lineages). Only given names are used.
- Day-of-week names (Dawa=Mon, Mingmar/Penpa=Tue, Lhakpa=Wed,
  Phurbu=Thu, Pasang=Fri, Pemba=Sat, Nyima=Sun) are extremely
  common and assigned by day of birth.
- Many names are unisex (marked m/f in dataset).
- Religious/Buddhist names (Tenzin, Lobsang, Thubten, Pema) are
  very common and often given by lamas.
- Tenzin is considered the single most common name in the Tibetan
  exile community due to the 14th Dalai Lama's influence.
- Compound names (e.g., Tenzin Gyatso, Lobsang Tenzin) are the norm;
  the dataset records individual name elements.
