UZBEK GIVEN NAMES — SOURCE INFORMATION
=========================================
Language family: Turkic (Karluk/Southeastern branch); distinct from Kazakh (Kipchak)
                 and Turkish (Oghuz). Closest relative: Uyghur.
Scripts: Uzbek Latin alphabet (official since 1993, fully adopted ~2000s),
         Cyrillic (still widely used in practice), Arabic (historical)
Population: ~36 million (2024)

NOTE ON NAMING PATTERNS: Uzbekistan's naming patterns show two distinct layers:
(1) Russian/Soviet-era names (Timur/Rustam/Elena/Tatyana) dominating all-time
    population statistics in the Forebears data.
(2) Contemporary Islamic names strongly dominating newborn data (2023+):
    Muhammad variants overwhelmingly dominate male names; Islamic female names
    (Soliha, Muslima, Hadicha, Imona) dominate female rankings.
    In 2023, 501,577 boys and 466,563 girls were born (total ~968,140 births).

KEY ANALYTICAL NOTES
------------------------------------------------------------
- For contemporary Uzbek naming culture, the 2023 newborn data is far more
  representative than the Forebears all-time data (which reflects Soviet era).
- Male naming is dominated by Muhammad + compounds: Muhammadali, Muhammadyusuf,
  Muhammadmin, Muhammadjon, Muhammadziyo are all distinct names in the top 10.
  This reflects strong post-Soviet Islamic revival in naming practices.
- Female names: Soliha (Arabic: virtuous/righteous), Yasmina (Arabic: jasmine),
  Muslima (Arabic: Muslim woman), Hadicha (Arabic: Khadija), Imona (Arabic: faith).
  All top-10 female names are Arabic/Islamic origin.
- The Uzbek script situation: official Latin script exists but Cyrillic remains
  common in practice; datasets use various romanisation conventions.
  The Ministry of Justice uses Uzbek Latin script officially.
- To obtain the full Forebears top-408 dataset for Uzbekistan, visit:
  https://forebears.io/uzbekistan/forenames and manually extract or scrape
  the full table (only top 10 was reliably extracted in this collection session).
