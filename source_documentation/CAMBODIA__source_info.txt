LINGUISTIC NOTES
===========================================================
- Khmer (Cambodian) is the official language of Cambodia
- It belongs to the Austroasiatic family, Mon-Khmer branch
- Written in the Khmer script (abugida), one of the oldest scripts in Southeast Asia
- All names in saved files are romanized transliterations
- Khmer romanization is not standardized; the same name may appear in multiple
  spellings (e.g., Sophal/Sopheal, Chantha/Chanthar)
- Cambodian given names are typically selected by monks or astrologers based on
  the day/time of birth; many are derived from Sanskrit/Pali via Hinduism/Buddhism
- Names often reflect auspicious meanings: Sokha (good health), Sophea (wisdom),
  Samnang (lucky), Ratana (jewel/gem), Vanna (gold)
- Common name elements: Sok- (happiness, prosperity), -na/-nang (feminine suffix),
  Chan- (moon), Sri-/Srey- (female honorific, beautiful)
- Cambodia's high name diversity (204,935 unique names) partly reflects the
  tradition of unique or semi-unique name assignment rather than names being
  recycled across families
- The Mon-Khmer branch is distinct from Vietnamese (also Austroasiatic but a
  different sub-branch, Vietic) which is already in the dataset
