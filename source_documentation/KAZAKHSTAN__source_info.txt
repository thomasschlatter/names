KAZAKH GIVEN NAMES — SOURCE INFORMATION
=========================================
Language family: Turkic (Kipchak branch); distinct from Turkish (Oghuz branch)
Scripts: Cyrillic (current official script), Kazakh Latin alphabet (transition in progress),
         Arabic script (historical; still used in diaspora communities in China)
Population: ~19.2 million (2024)

NOTE ON NAMING PATTERNS: Kazakhstan is ethnically mixed (~70% Kazakh, ~18% Russian
as of recent censuses). This is clearly visible in the Forebears data, where Russian
names (Elena, Sergey, Natalya) dominate the all-time counts due to Soviet-era naming,
while the newborn data (Bureau of National Statistics) shows the rapid shift toward
Kazakh and Islamic names in the contemporary period.

------------------------------------------------------------
KEY ANALYTICAL NOTES
------------------------------------------------------------
- The top 1,000 Forebears names are MIXED (Kazakh + Russian) reflecting
  demographic history. For Kazakh-specific names, filter by names not
  obviously Russian/Slavic (Elena, Sergey, etc.).
- Newborn data (2023-2025) is far more representative of contemporary
  Kazakh naming: strongly Islamic (Muhammad variants, Ali, Omar) for boys;
  Turkic-Islamic blend for girls (Medina, Aisha, Tomiris, Ailin).
- The name Tomiris refers to the legendary Massagetae queen and represents
  a wave of historically-inspired Kazakh naming.
- Kazakhstan is mid-transition from Cyrillic to Latin script; official
  romanisation rules are still being standardised (hence variation:
  Alikhan / Alihan, Ailin / Ailyn).
